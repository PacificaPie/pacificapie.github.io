ResourceLoop — a B-Side System Defined and Shipped from Zero ResourceLoop —— 从零定义并交付的 B 端系统

Internship · Meituan Internal Applications
internship project (internal)  ·  I defined the workflow, data model, interaction, and incentive mechanism; the team validated, and we co-developed pairing with AI 实习项目（内部）  ·  我定义工作流、数据模型、交互与激励机制，团队校验，和 AI 结对协同开发

The problem

Trending events in travel — concerts, matches, festivals — need BD teams to claim platform resources to support them. There was no systematic way to do it: the request-and-approval loop lived nowhere in particular, and no one could see which requests were actually worth granting.

My role & the collaboration model

I defined the whole thing from zero — the workflow, the data model, the interaction, the incentive mechanism, and the acceptance criteria. The team validated the operating assumptions; we co-developed the implementation, pairing with AI for prototyping. Working with AI has to be driven by your own structured thinking: a PM can build, but never at the cost of time spent thinking about direction.

System architecture

Three role consoles — request, review, deploy — sit on a realtime Postgres BaaS with row-level security, realtime subscriptions, and SSO role mapping. Underneath are four domain engines: a success-rate engine, a heat model, an LLM material recommender, and a two-stage image generator, each reading and writing its own tables. One rule shaped the backend: the success rate is computed once, on the server — the single authoritative source of truth. The frontend went from double-computing to a thin consumer with a defensive fallback, so when the pricing model was later upgraded from two tiers to three, the frontend didn't change a line.

Mechanism design

The system isn't a form — it's a small market, so I designed it like one. Mechanism design means taking “how will people game this?” as a first-class input.

  • Success rates are semi-visible — enough to guide a request, not enough to reverse-engineer and game for arbitrage.
  • Appeals are decoupled from review, and reviewers carry a scorecard — the power to reject comes with matching accountability. Two-way, not one.
  • One submission per event; cards stay locked to an event ID across days, so the same event can't be quietly double-claimed.

Every item on the “won't build” list has a concrete scenario behind it — landing pages, withdrawals, client-side ordering, frontend allow-lists. I kept the surface small on purpose.

Modeling heat as a latent variable

Which events deserve resources? Heat is a latent variable — there's no clean ground truth for it. So I didn't pretend there was. An LLM produces first-pass labels, pairwise comparison calibrates them into a consistent ordering, a supervised model learns from that, and its output drives the request-and-selection engine. The discipline is admitting where truth is manufactured — and building the calibration that makes it trustworthy.

LLM material recommendation

When a BD claims an event, the system suggests matching support materials. Two product calls shaped it. First, the LLM's job here is to widen the consideration set, not to decide — the output is a candidate list a human checks off, never a verdict. Second, evaluation comes before optimization: without a fixed sample, “it feels better” is worthless.

Seven prompt versions on 103 real events, each compared head-to-head against the last. The lesson was in a badcase: a Mongolian artist's concert in a stadium once produced grassland shuttle buses — the model doing surface association after its examples were stripped of their reasoning paths. Restore the reasoning, add counter-examples, and that whole class of error disappears.

Two-stage image generation

Every event card needs a banner, and AI image models garbling Chinese text is an industry-wide problem. I read it as a pipeline problem, not a wording one: any title string that enters a text-to-image prompt gets rendered as picture text. So a text model first translates the event into a pure visual scene — stripped of every name and word — and only then hits the image model; titles are composed in the DOM afterward, cleanly. Generation runs offline and batched, and the frontend only ever reads the table, so a slow model call never sits on a user request. Even the style was weakness-driven: impressionist brushwork, because it hides the model's detail slips and matches the brand palette — choosing to conceal, not to show off.

Engineering discipline

Product depth here meant engineering discipline, four ways:

  • Single source of truth — I proposed the success-rate algorithm, the team validated it, then I pushed the sub-scores to close on the backend, handing over a four-piece package: a JS reference, a Java reference, a numeric oracle, and a README.
  • Contract governance — the interface spec shipped three versions in a week; each one I diffed into an integration checklist, froze a snapshot on submit to preserve historical semantics, and treated units (cents vs. yuan) as part of the contract.
  • No silent failures — a core relational field was being written as an empty array, silently, breaking the relationship; I traced it layer by layer and made it a rule: never downgrade an exception into a valid-looking empty value. Number(null) === 0 got caught three separate times by tests — language traps are stopped by tests, not by discipline.
  • An engineering baseline — I ran a ~4,300-line full review, graded findings P0–P3, and sequenced a roadmap by “probability of breaking × how fast you'd notice.” Vitest, react-query, route splitting, and lint all landed — lint caught a real bug the moment it went in; a 918-line file became seven, behavior-unchanged, triple-verified.

Then I compiled the recurring parts into engineering-method skills — a reusable methodology wiki, so the next person doesn't relearn it from scratch.

On-site pilot & two-sided research

Then I ran the pilot on-site, solo: briefing dozens of BDs face-to-face, taking their requests and shipping front- and back-end changes live, so propose → build → feedback closed right on the floor. The interaction model was rebuilt from that feedback — a Gantt accordion torn down for an event calendar with a pinned request zone, explicit draft-state backfill, and error messages rewritten in plain human language for non-engineer users. Alongside it I designed and ran two-sided research — BD and merchant — using the prototype as an interview stimulus, quota self-checks to keep the sample representative, and classification by objective orders rather than self-report. It converged on three corrections to the product direction.

What iterating taught me

Working with AI has to be driven by your own structured thinking. A PM can take part in development — but never at the cost of time spent thinking about the big direction.

A merchant's offhand “I can just ask an AI for this” reframed the endgame for me: the moat was never a trending-events calendar — it's the closed loop of information, resources, and outcome feedback.

问题

酒旅的热点事件 —— 演唱会、赛事、节庆 —— 需要 BD 申请平台资源来承接，此前没有系统化流程： 申请与审批的循环无处安放，也没人看得清哪些申请真正值得批。

我的角色与协作模型

我从零定义了整套东西 —— 工作流、数据模型、交互、激励机制与验收标准。团队校验业务假设， 我们借助 AI 的原型能力协同实现。与 AI 协同必须用自己的结构化思考带动：产品可以参与开发， 但绝不能牺牲对大方向的思考时间。

系统架构

三个角色台 —— 申报、审核、投放 —— 跑在一套实时 Postgres BaaS 之上，配行级权限（RLS）、 实时订阅与 SSO 角色映射。下面是四个领域引擎：成功率引擎、热度模型、LLM 物料推荐、 两段式生图，各自读写自己的表。有一条规矩塑造了整个后端： 成功率只在服务端算一次 —— 权威唯一来源。前端从「双重计算」瘦身为 「消费 + 防御性兜底」，所以后来定价体系从两价升到三价时，前端一行没改。

机制设计

这套系统不是一张表单 —— 它是个小市场，所以我按市场来设计。机制设计的第一性输入， 是「人会怎么钻空子」。

  • 成功率半外显 —— 足以指导一次申报，又不足以被逆向、被拿去套利刷单。
  • 申诉与审核解耦，审核员带记分卡 —— 驳回的权力配上对应的问责。 双向，不是单向。
  • 一事件一次提交；卡片按事件 ID 跨天一致锁定，同一事件无法被悄悄重复申报。

「不做」清单上每一条都有具体场景撑着 —— landing 页、撤回、前端排序、前端白名单。 我是刻意把暴露面保持得小。

把热度当潜变量建模

哪些事件值得给资源？热度是个潜变量 —— 它没有干净的真值。所以我没假装它有。 由 LLM 产出首轮标签，用 pairwise 比较把它们校准成一致的排序， 监督学习模型再从中学习，其输出驱动招选引擎。纪律在于：承认真值是被制造出来的 —— 再把让它可信的那层校准搭起来。

LLM 物料推荐

BD 申报某事件时，系统会推荐配套物料。两个产品级判断。其一，LLM 在这里的职责是 扩大考虑集，而不是替人拍板 —— 输出是 BD 勾选的候选清单，不是标准答案。 其二，评测先于优化：没有固定样本，「感觉变好了」一文不值。

7 个 prompt 版本、103 条真实事件、每版对上一版逐条对照。教训藏在一个 badcase 里： 「蒙古族艺人在体育馆开演唱会」曾输出「草原接驳车」 —— 示例被砍掉推理路径后，模型做了形似关联。补回推理路径、加上反面示例，这一类错误就整类消失。

两段式事件配图

每张事件卡都要一张 banner，而 AI 生图把中文渲染成乱码是行业通病。我把它读成 链路结构问题，而非措辞问题：任何标题字符串一旦进入文生图 prompt， 就会被当画面文字画出来。于是让文本模型先把事件翻译成纯画面场景 —— 剥掉所有名称和文字 —— 再喂给图像模型；标题交给前端 DOM 事后叠加，天然清晰。生成离线批量跑，前端只读表， 几十秒的模型调用绝不挂在用户请求上。连画风都是「弱点驱动选型」：选印象派笔触， 因为它天然掩盖模型的细节翻车、又贴品牌色板 —— 是藏拙，不是炫技。

工程纪律

这里的产品深度，落成四条工程纪律：

  • 权威唯一来源 —— 成功率算法我先提出、团队校验落地，之后我推动子分数 闭环到后端，交付一套四件套：JS 参考版、Java 参考版、数值对照 oracle、README。
  • 契约治理 —— 接口文档一周三版；每版我都 diff 成对接清单，提交时冻结快照 以保住历史口径，并把单位（分 vs 元）当作契约的一部分。
  • 不静默吞错 —— 一个核心关联字段被静默写成空数组、关系因此断裂； 我逐层定位后立下规矩：不许把异常静默降级成看起来合法的空值。 Number(null) === 0 被单测三次逮到 —— 语言陷阱靠测试防，不靠纪律。
  • 工程化基线 —— 我发起约 4,300 行的全量 review，P0–P3 分级， 按「改坏概率 × 能否立即发现」排序路线图。vitest、react-query、路由分包、lint 全部落地 —— lint 补上当场就拦到一个真 bug；918 行的大文件拆成 7 个，行为零变更、三重验证。

最后我把其中反复出现的部分固化成研发方法论 skills —— 一份可复用的方法论 wiki， 让下一个人不必从头重学。

驻场试点与双侧调研

随后我以「一人驻场」跑通试点：面向数十名 BD 现场宣讲答疑、当场承接需求改前后端即时上线， 让提出 → 实现 → 反馈在现场闭环。交互模型正是从这些反馈里重建的 —— 甘特手风琴被推翻、换成事件日历 + 钉死申报区，草稿态显式回填，报错信息为非工程用户 改成人话外显。同时我设计并执行了双侧（BD／商家）调研 —— 以产品原型作访谈刺激物、 配额自查保样本代表性、按客观订单归类而非用户自称 —— 收敛出三处产品方向修正。

认知迭代

与 AI 协同要用结构化思考带动；产品可以参与开发，但不能牺牲对大方向的思考时间。

商家一句「这些我用 AI 一问就有」点破了产品终局：壁垒从来不是热点日历， 而是「信息 + 资源 + 效果回流」的闭环。
